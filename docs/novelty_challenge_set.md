# Five strongest novelty challenges — provisional set

1. **FORGE (2026, Nature Communications)** — directly evaluates expression, SNP, and CNV contributions and reports expression dominance. This is the closest challenge to the modality-value premise.
2. **DrEval (2026)** — provides broad benchmark coverage, realistic splits, randomization-based modality ablation, and cross-study evaluation. This challenges generic claims about evaluation novelty.
3. **MKDR (2025)** — studies missing multi-omics modalities and modality completion, although in a narrower cervical-cancer setting and with a more complex architecture.
4. **Menden et al., A cross-study analysis of drug response prediction in cancer cell lines (2022)** — evaluates five pharmacogenomic screens and partial-data/cross-study generalization. This challenges cross-dataset novelty.
5. **Haibe-Kains et al., Inconsistency in large pharmacogenomic studies (2013), with subsequent reproducibility analyses** — establishes that assay and protocol differences can dominate apparent biological agreement. This challenges naive interpretations of external validation.

## Conservative novelty statement

The proposed contribution is not a new multimodal architecture or the first modality-ablation study. Subject to completion of the formal review, the remaining gap appears to be a pre-specified, pharmacology-aware estimation of modality-specific incremental value across mechanisms of action, combined with explicit missingness, conflict/redundancy, calibration, and cross-study measurement sensitivity analyses using simple reproducible models.

This statement remains provisional until full bibliographic verification and methods-level comparison are complete.
